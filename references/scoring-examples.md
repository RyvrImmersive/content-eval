# Scoring Examples & Calibration Guide

Use these annotated examples to calibrate scores when a dimension feels ambiguous.

---

## Clarity

**Score 9:** Every sentence has one job. Technical terms are defined on first use.
Paragraphs are ≤4 sentences. The reader never has to re-read a sentence to understand it.
> Example: "We reduced churn by 34% in 90 days. Here's the three-step playbook we used."

**Score 6:** Mostly readable but with recurring throat-clearing, passive voice overuse,
or jargon that assumes too much.
> Example: "In the context of leveraging our go-to-market synergies across the enterprise
> landscape, optimising for customer lifetime value remains a strategic imperative."

**Score 3:** Dense, meandering sentences. Ideas buried in subordinate clauses. Reader
frequently loses the thread.

---

## Credibility

**Score 9:** Author credentials are stated or well-known. Claims are backed by named
studies with years and organisations. Original research is presented. The brand has a
clear, demonstrated stake in the topic.

**Score 6:** Some data cited but sources are vague ("studies show," "research indicates").
Author bio is absent or generic. Claims are plausible but unverifiable.

**Score 3:** No citations. Assertions presented as fact. No author context. Reads like
marketing copy dressed as thought leadership.

---

## Originality

**Score 9:** Offers a proprietary framework, counter-intuitive finding, or a named
point of view the reader hasn't seen before. The piece adds something to the conversation.

**Score 5:** Competent summary of well-known ideas. Well-written but says nothing new.
Could have been written by anyone in the industry.

**Score 2:** Listicle of generic best practices. Could be found on the first page of
a Google search. No differentiated perspective.

---

## Evidence Quality

**Score 10:** Original primary research (surveys, proprietary data, experiments).
Methodology disclosed. Sample sizes stated.

**Score 7:** Credible third-party research cited (Gartner, McKinsey, academic papers)
with year and specific finding. Named customer examples with measurable outcomes.

**Score 4:** Generic industry statistics without source, or anecdotes without
quantification. "Many companies find that..." type evidence.

**Score 1:** Pure assertion. No data, no examples, no citations.

---

## CTA Effectiveness

**Score 9:** The CTA flows naturally from the content's argument. It's specific,
low-friction, and gives the reader a clear reason to act now.
> Example (whitepaper): "Download our ROI calculator — it uses the same methodology
> we applied to reduce [Client]'s CAC by 40%."

**Score 5:** CTA exists but feels bolted on. Generic ("Contact us to learn more").
No connection to what was just argued.

**Score 1:** No CTA, or CTA is buried/hidden.
